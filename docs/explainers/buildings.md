# How tall can a building be?

![Tallest buildings over time](../../figures/buildings.png)

The Great Pyramid (146.6 m) was the tallest structure on Earth for about 3,800 years. Iron and steel broke that record in the 1880s. The record then nearly tripled in 120 years, from the Eiffel Tower (300 m, 1889) to the Burj Khalifa (828 m, 2010).

**Limits.**
- **Crushing height (dotted amber).** A uniform column crushes its own base when ρgh exceeds its compressive strength. That happens at about 1.7 km for 40 MPa concrete and 4.6 km for structural steel. Tapering beats this: a pyramid-shaped tower carries less weight at each level.
- **Mountains (dashed grey).** Rock flows under its own weight at around 10 km on Earth (Weisskopf 1975), which is why Everest is about as tall as mountains get here.

In practice wind, sway, elevator travel and cost stop towers long before materials do.
