# Limits of the human mind

![Human cognitive limits](../../figures/cognition.png)

## a. How fast do we process?

The senses take in about 10⁹ bits per second. The retina alone sends about 10⁷ bits per second down the optic nerve. Yet every behaviour measured so far runs at about **10 bits per second**: typing, speed-reading, memorising cards, playing StarCraft (Zheng & Meister 2025). Speech carries about 39 bits per second in every language studied (Coupé et al. 2019).

The hard bound is thermodynamic. Erasing one bit costs at least k_BT ln 2 of energy (Landauer 1961). At 20 W and 310 K, a brain could erase at most about 7×10²¹ bits per second. Behaviour runs twenty orders of magnitude below that. The 10 bits/s limit comes from how the brain works, not from physics.

## b. How much can we hold?

- **At once:** about 4 chunks in working memory (Cowan 2001), or 7 ± 2 items in the classic span task (Miller 1956).
- **Socially:** about 150 stable relationships (Dunbar 1992) and about 5,000 known faces (Jenkins et al. 2018).
- **In long-term memory:** about 42,000 word lemmas (Brysbaert et al. 2016), and 10,000 pictures recognised after a single viewing (Standing 1973).
- **Hardware:** 86 billion neurons and about 1.5×10¹⁴ neocortical synapses.
