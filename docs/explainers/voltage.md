# How much voltage can a gap hold?

![Voltage vs gap](../../figures/voltage.png)

**Bounds.**
- **Schwinger field (red, sloped).** Above 1.3×10¹⁸ V/m, empty space breaks down into electron-positron pairs. No static field can be sustained beyond it, so a gap d holds at most E_S·d.
- **Planck voltage (red, flat).** A charged body at potential V stores field energy that grows with V². Once V reaches c²/√(4πε₀G) ≈ 10²⁷ V, that energy fits inside its own Schwarzschild radius, whatever the size. Gravity sets this ceiling. It meets the Schwinger line at gaps of about 8×10⁸ m, so beyond the size of the Sun gravity, not QED, limits the voltage.

**Practical limit.** Air sparks at about 3 MV/m (dotted amber), which sets the length of a lightning bolt or a spark.

**Objects.**
- **Hydrogen atom:** the electron sits in 5×10¹¹ V/m.
- **Mitochondria:** 150–180 mV across a 5 nm membrane drives ATP synthesis.
- **Nerve cell membrane:** 70 mV across 7 nm, about 10 million V/m, three times the field that makes air spark.
- **Static shock:** up to 15 kV jumps a few millimetres from a fingertip.
- **Standing in fair weather:** about 200 V between head and feet.
- **Electric eel:** delivers 860 V across a two-metre body.
- **Laser-plasma accelerator:** gains 8 GeV in 20 cm.
- **Lightning:** hundreds of megavolts over kilometres.
- **Global atmospheric circuit:** keeps the ionosphere about 250 kV above the ground.
- **Polar cap:** the solar wind drives tens to hundreds of kilovolts across the auroral oval.
- **Io:** moving through Jupiter's field makes the moon a 0.4 MV dynamo.
- **Pulsar polar caps:** reach 10¹²–10¹⁶ V, enough to tear particles off the star.
- **Spinning supermassive black holes:** a 10⁸ solar-mass hole in a 1 T field makes about 10²⁰ V (Blandford–Znajek). That is the largest voltage known, and it is still ten million times below the Planck voltage.
