# How much can flow through a channel?

![Volumetric flow vs channel size](../../figures/flow.png)

Flow through a channel of width d is speed times area, Q = v·πd²/4.

**Hard bound.** The flow cannot move faster than light, so Q < c·πd²/4 (red).

**Practical limit.** A liquid pushed faster than its own speed of sound (1,482 m/s for water) shocks and cavitates (dotted amber). Real pipes flow at a few metres per second (dash-dot: 1 m/s).

**Objects.**
- Plumbing: a bathroom tap (EPA WaterSense limit of 1.5 gallons per minute) and a shower.
- The human aorta carries about 5 litres a minute.
- The Amazon discharges about 210,000 m³/s, a sixth of all river water reaching the oceans. The Congo carries 41,000 m³/s, the Mississippi 19,000 and the Danube 6,400 (Dai & Trenberth 2002; channel widths approximate).
- Ocean currents are measured in sverdrups (10⁶ m³/s). The Florida Current carries about 32 Sv. The Antarctic Circumpolar Current carries 173 Sv through Drake Passage, more than all the world's rivers combined 150 times over. The Atlantic overturning circulation carries 17 Sv north across the whole basin at 26° N, along with 1.25 PW of heat.
