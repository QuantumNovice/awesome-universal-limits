# How long is a genome, and how many genes does it hold?

![Genome size vs genes](../../figures/genomes.png)

The bottom axis gives genome length in base pairs. The top axis gives the length of that DNA stretched out (0.34 nm per base pair): a human cell holds about 1 m of it per genome copy.

**Lines.**
- **One gene per 1,000 bp (dotted amber).** Viruses and bacteria pack genes this tightly, with almost no space between them. Some viruses beat it by overlapping genes.
- **One gene per 100,000 bp (dash-dot).** Large animal and plant genomes approach this.
- **Largest known genome (dashed, vertical).** The fork fern *Tmesipteris oblanceolata* has 160 billion base pairs, 50 times the human genome.

**The C-value paradox.** Above about 10⁸ bp, adding DNA stops adding genes. Humans, axolotls and lungfish all have 20,000–30,000 protein-coding genes, while their genomes differ fifteen-fold. The extra DNA is mostly repeats and non-coding sequence.

**The small end.** The insect symbiont Nasuia has 137 genes, and the synthetic minimal cell JCVI-syn3.0 has 438 protein-coding genes. These hint at the smallest gene set life needs.
