# Pulsars and magnetars: how fast do they spin down?

![P-Pdot diagram](../../figures/pulsars.png)

A pulsar is a spinning, magnetised neutron star. It slows down as it radiates. Its period P and slow-down rate Ṗ reveal its magnetic field, B ≈ 3.2×10¹⁵ T √(PṖ), and its characteristic age, τ = P/(2Ṗ).

**Hard bound.** A 25 km neutron star cannot spin faster than its rim moving at light speed: P > πd/c ≈ 0.26 ms (blue, shaded). The fastest known pulsar, at 1.4 ms, is about five times slower, because it would fly apart well before reaching light speed.

**Lines.**
- *Quantum critical field* (4.4×10⁹ T, dashed grey). Above it, an electron's cyclotron energy exceeds its rest energy. Magnetars live up here: SGR 1806-20 has the strongest field known.
- *Ages* (dotted amber, 1,000 years). Swift J1818.0-1607 is only a few hundred years old.
- *Age of the universe* (red, dashed). Recycled millisecond pulsars sit near it: they spin down so slowly they will keep ticking for billions of years.
