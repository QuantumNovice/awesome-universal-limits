# How long can things last?

![Lifetime vs size](../../figures/lifetime.png)

**Hard bound.** No physical process is shorter than the Planck time (5.4×10⁻⁴⁴ s, blue).

**The age of the universe (red, dashed, unshaded).** Nothing has been *watched* for longer than 13.8 billion years. Longer lifetimes are still real, but they are inferred from decay rates: xenon-124 has a half-life a trillion times the age of the universe, measured by counting a few rare decays in a tonne of xenon. This line is a limit on observation, not on existence, so the region above it is not shaded.

**Reference lines.** The dash-dot line is how long a black hole of diameter *d* takes to evaporate by Hawking radiation (t ∝ d³). The grey dashed line is the light-crossing time d/c.

**Objects.** From the top quark (5×10⁻²⁵ s, too short-lived to form hadrons) and the Higgs boson, through muons, free neutrons and E. coli generations, to humans, Greenland sharks (about 400 years), bristlecone pines (about 4,900 years), the Sun and red dwarfs. The proton point is a **lower limit**: Super-Kamiokande has watched for decays and seen none, so protons live at least 2.4×10³⁴ years.

Point particles have no size, so they are plotted at their Compton wavelength h/(mc).
