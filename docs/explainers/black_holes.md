# How fast do black holes spin?

![Spin vs mass](../../figures/black_holes.png)

A black hole is fully described by its mass, spin and charge. Spin is measured by the dimensionless parameter a = Jc/(GM²).

**Hard bound.** a ≤ 1. A faster-spinning Kerr solution has no event horizon and would expose a naked singularity. Penrose's cosmic censorship conjecture says nature forbids this (red).

**Practical limit.** Thorne (1974) showed that a disk feeding a hole also absorbs radiation that slows it down, so accretion stalls at a ≈ 0.998 (dotted amber).

**Measurements.** X-ray binaries span the whole range, from A0620-00 (a ≈ 0.12) to Cygnus X-1 (a > 0.9985, right against the limit). Holes formed by mergers settle near a ≈ 0.7, as the orbital angular momentum of the merging pair predicts. GW190521's 142 M☉ remnant is the first clear intermediate-mass black hole.
