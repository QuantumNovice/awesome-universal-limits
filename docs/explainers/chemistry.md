# How tightly can matter hold together?

![Binding energy vs size](../../figures/chemistry.png)

This chart puts the energy needed to pull a system apart against its size. It spans the helium dimer (bound by 150 neV across 47 Å) to the uranium nucleus (1.8 GeV in 15 fm).

**Reference lines.**
- **Coulomb energy e²/(4πε₀d) (dash-dot).** The energy scale of two unit charges at distance *d*. Chemical bonds and atomic binding energies sit near or below it: hydrogen's 13.6 eV ionisation energy lands exactly on it at two Bohr radii. Nuclei sit far above it, because the strong force, not electromagnetism, holds them together.
- **Electron confinement h²/(8m_e d²) (dashed grey).** The kinetic energy cost of squeezing an electron into a box of width *d*. Atoms are sized where this cost balances Coulomb attraction, which is why the two lines cross near 1 Å.
- **Room-temperature thermal energy k_BT (dotted amber).** Bonds weaker than about 0.026 eV are shaken apart at 300 K. Van der Waals dimers like Ar₂ and He₂ exist only in the cold. Hydrogen bonds sit just above the line, which is why water is a liquid.

**Objects.** He₂ and Ar₂ (van der Waals), the water dimer (hydrogen bond), NaCl (ionic), C–C, C=C and C≡C bonds, H₂, N₂, CO (the strongest chemical bond, 11.1 eV), ionisation of hydrogen and of a Rydberg atom, and the deuteron, iron-56 and uranium-238 nuclei.
