# How much energy can a kilogram hold?

![Energy per kilogram](../../figures/energy_density.png)

**Hard bound.** E = mc²: converting a whole kilogram into energy gives 9×10¹⁶ J. Nothing can store or release more per kilogram (red). Matter meeting antimatter reaches it; everything else stops far short.

**The ladder.**
- **Batteries** hold 0.1–1 MJ/kg. Lithium-ion cells sit at the top of electrochemical storage, about 20 times more than a supercapacitor.
- **Chemical fuels** hold about 50 times more than batteries: 44 MJ/kg for gasoline and 120 MJ/kg for hydrogen. Batteries are lighter on the whole system because engines waste most of a fuel's energy, but per kilogram, fuel wins.
- **Nuclear fission** of uranium-235 releases about 82 TJ/kg, two million times gasoline. Deuterium-tritium fusion gives about four times more again.
- **Accretion onto a spinning black hole** is the most efficient process known. Matter spiralling in can radiate up to 32% of its rest energy (42% for a maximally spinning hole).

Pumped hydro stores only a few kJ/kg, but water is cheap and reservoirs are huge.
