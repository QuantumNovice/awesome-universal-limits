# How much electric charge can an object carry?

![Charge vs size](../../figures/charge.png)

**Hard bounds.**
- **Quantum floor (blue).** Free charges come in whole multiples of the elementary charge e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C. Quarks carry thirds of e but are never seen alone. Millikan's oil drops (1913) always carried whole numbers of e.
- **Gravity ceiling (red).** An electric field stores energy, and energy gravitates. Squeeze enough charge onto a sphere of radius r and the energy of its own field, Q²/(8πε₀r), would lie inside its Schwarzschild radius. This happens once the Reissner–Nordström charge radius √(GQ²/4πε₀)/c² exceeds r, so Q < r c² √(4πε₀/G), about 10¹⁷ coulombs per metre of radius. The ceiling grows only in proportion to size, while the Schwinger line grows with the square of size. Gravity therefore takes over from QED as the true ceiling above about 10⁹ m, which is the size of the Sun.

**Where QED and gravity meet.** The Planck charge √(4πε₀ħc) ≈ 11.7 e (dash-dot) is the charge whose Coulomb energy at any distance equals ħc/r. It is e/√α, so the fine-structure constant α ≈ 1/137 is the statement that the elementary charge is about a twelfth of the Planck charge.

**Practical limits.**
- **Schwinger limit (dashed grey).** At 1.3×10¹⁸ V/m the vacuum itself breaks down into electron-positron pairs. Nuclei exceed this field at their surface, but only over a region smaller than the electron's Compton wavelength, so no pairs are made. A bare nucleus with Z above about 173 would spark the vacuum.
- **Air breakdown (dotted amber).** A charged sphere in air sparks once its surface field reaches about 3 MV/m. Lightning, Van de Graaff generators and charged people live on this line.
- **Rayleigh limit (dashed amber).** A charged drop flies apart when electric pressure beats surface tension: q = 8π√(ε₀γr³). Duft and colleagues (2003) filmed a levitated drop shooting out jets at exactly this limit.

**Objects.**
- An interplanetary dust grain loses photoelectrons to sunlight until it sits at about +5 V, which is a few thousand elementary charges.
- Storm raindrops carry up to a few picocoulombs, a hundred times below their Rayleigh limit.
- A person who has just walked across a carpet holds a couple of microcoulombs at up to 15 kV.
- The ATS-6 satellite charged to −19 kV in Earth's shadow.
- Each LHC beam carries 52 µC of protons around a 27 km ring.
- A thundercloud's main negative region holds about 40 C, and one lightning stroke lowers about 5 C.
- The Earth carries about 450,000 C on its surface, which makes the fair-weather field of 100 V/m.
- The Sun is slightly positive, at about 77 C, because electrons escape it more easily than protons.
- The Milky Way's black hole, Sagittarius A*, carries at most 3×10⁸ C. That is 10¹⁹ times below the charge that would make it an extremal black hole.
