# How much electric charge can an object carry?

![Charge vs size](../../figures/charge.png)

**Hard bound.** Free charges come in whole multiples of the elementary charge e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C (blue). Quarks carry thirds of e but are never seen alone. Millikan's oil drops (1913) always carried whole numbers of e.

**Practical limits.**
- **Air breakdown (dotted amber).** A charged sphere in air sparks once its surface field reaches about 3 MV/m. Lightning and Van de Graaff generators live on this line.
- **Schwinger limit (dashed grey).** At 1.3×10¹⁸ V/m, the vacuum itself breaks down into electron-positron pairs. Nuclei exceed this field at their surface, but only over a region smaller than the electron's Compton wavelength, so no pairs are made. A charge Z ≳ 170 would spark the vacuum.

**Objects.**
- The Earth carries about 450,000 C on its surface, which makes the fair-weather field of 100 V/m.
- The Sun is slightly positive, at about 77 C, because electrons escape it more easily than protons.
