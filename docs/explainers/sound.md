# How loud can a sound be?

![Sound levels](../../figures/sound.png)

A sound wave is a pressure ripple around the ambient pressure.

**Hard bound in air.** The low-pressure trough of a wave cannot drop below vacuum. With 1 atm of air, the largest undistorted wave has an amplitude of 101,325 Pa: 194 dB re 20 µPa (red). Louder events are shock waves, not sound.

**Underwater** the reference pressure is 1 µPa, so the numbers are not comparable with air. Near the surface the same limit is about 220 dB, beyond which water cavitates (dotted amber). Deeper water has higher ambient pressure and allows louder sounds. That is how sperm whales, hunting hundreds of metres down, produce 236 dB clicks, the loudest sounds made by any animal. A snapping shrimp's claw collapses a cavitation bubble to make its 218 dB snap.

**Exposure limits.** Hearing starts near 0 dB. NIOSH recommends no more than 85 dBA over 8 hours and never more than 140 dB peak. A gunshot at the shooter's ear exceeds that. The loudest animal in air is a fishing bat: its echolocation calls reach 137–140 dB at 10 cm.
