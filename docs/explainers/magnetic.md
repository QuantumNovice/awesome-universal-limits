# How strong can a magnetic field be?

![Magnetic field vs size](../../figures/magnetic.png)

**Hard bound.** A magnetic field stores energy B²/(2μ₀) per cubic metre. Pack enough of it into a region of size d and the field's own energy forms a black hole. That gives B < √(3μ₀/πG)·c²/d (red), about 10¹⁹ T for a one-metre region.

**Lines.**
- **Quantum critical field (dashed grey).** At 4.4×10⁹ T, an electron's lowest orbit in the field carries more energy than its rest mass. Atoms are squeezed into needles and light can split in two. Magnetars live above it.
- **Strongest steady lab field (dotted amber).** 45.5 T, from a high-temperature superconducting insert magnet.

**Objects.**
- **Human brain:** femtotesla signals.
- **Interstellar field:** about 5×10⁻¹⁰ T.
- **Earth:** 25–65 µT.
- **Sunspots:** a few tenths of a tesla.
- **MRI scanners:** up to 11.7 T.
- **Destructive lab pulse:** 1,200 T for microseconds.
- **White dwarfs:** up to 10⁵ T.
- **Pulsars and magnetars:** up to 2×10¹¹ T.
