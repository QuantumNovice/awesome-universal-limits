# How much do gas and water weigh?

![Mass vs volume](../../figures/volumes.png)

The lines are densities: water (1,000 kg/m³, dotted amber) and air at sea level (1.225 kg/m³, dash-dot).

**Hard bound.** A volume V whose mass exceeds c²d/(4G), with d = (6V/π)^(1/3), is inside its own horizon (red). Because mass grows as V while the bound grows only as V^(1/3), any fixed density eventually crosses it. A ball of water about 5 AU across (wider than Jupiter's orbit) would be a black hole, and so would a ball of sea-level air about 150 AU across. That is why giant gas clouds in space are so thin: a giant molecular cloud is 10¹⁹ times less dense than air.

**Objects.**
- One breath (half a litre).
- Lakes Superior and Baikal.
- The Antarctic ice sheet.
- All the oceans (1.33 billion km³).
- All of Earth's water.
- The atmosphere: 5.1×10¹⁸ kg, plotted at the volume it would fill if it were all at sea-level density.
- A giant molecular cloud.
