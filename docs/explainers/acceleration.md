# How hard can things accelerate?

![Acceleration vs size](../../figures/acceleration.png)

**Hard bounds.** Accelerate a rigid object of length *d* faster than a = c²/d and its trailing end would have to fall behind a Rindler horizon. No signal could keep the object in one piece (red). The same value is the surface gravity of a black hole of diameter *d*, so Sgr A* and M87* sit exactly on the line. The neutron star PSR J0740+6620 reaches about 70% of it. The Planck acceleration (5.6×10⁵¹ m/s²) caps everything.

**Observability floor (blue, dashed).** Below a = d/t₀², an object starting from rest would not have moved its own size since the Big Bang.

**Practical limits.** The dotted amber line is the rim acceleration of a rotor spinning at the material limit (rim speed 3 km/s, so a = 2v²/d). It ties this chart to the spin chart. The ultracentrifuge, at about a million g, sits just below it.

**Objects.** Biology is surprisingly extreme: a Hydra stinging cell fires at 5 million g, and a mantis shrimp strikes at 10,000 g. Pilots stay conscious up to about 9 g with a G-suit. The cap at 46 g is John Stapp's 1954 rocket-sled run. Laser-wakefield accelerators push electrons at about 7×10²¹ m/s².
