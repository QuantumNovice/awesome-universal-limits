# How fast can things move?

![Speed vs size](../../figures/speed.png)

**Hard bound.** Nothing with mass reaches the speed of light (red). LHC protons are only 2.9 m/s short of it, and the Oh-My-God cosmic ray missed it by about four parts in 10²⁴. On a log axis both look like they sit on the line. Neither touches it.

**Observability floor (blue, dashed).** Something that moves less than its own size in the age of the universe, v < d/t₀, has never visibly moved. Like the spin chart's floor, this is a limit on what we can see, not a law.

**Practical limits.** Sound can't travel faster than about 36 km/s in any solid or liquid. That bound comes from fundamental constants alone (Trachenko et al. 2020) and is drawn in dotted amber. The dash-dot line is the speed of sound in air, for scale.

**Objects.** The chart runs from swimming bacteria (tens of µm/s) through cheetahs, sprinters and the ThrustSSC car to Voyager 1, the Parker Solar Probe (192 km/s, the fastest human-made object), the star S2 at its closest pass to Sgr A* (2.5% of c) and the M87 jet. The cap above Earth is solar escape speed: past it, Earth would leave the Sun.
